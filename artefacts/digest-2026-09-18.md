## 1. What is photosynthesis and what are its main stages?

Photosynthesis is a system of biological processes used by photopigment-bearing autotrophic organisms, such as most plants, algae, and cyanobacteria, to convert light energy into chemical energy [1, 2]. Typically relying on sunlight, this process provides the chemical energy necessary to fuel the organisms' metabolism [2]. Oxygenic photosynthesis specifically releases oxygen as a byproduct through the splitting of water [2]. The converted chemical energy is then stored within the bonds of intracellular organic compounds, including carbohydrates like sugars, starches, phytoglycogen, and cellulose [2].

## 2. How do transformer-based language models handle long context windows?

Transformer-based language models use attention, a mathematical operation that compares the latest token to every previous token, allowing them to recall information by "remembering" every token in their context [4]. However, this means conventional models become less efficient and slower as the context grows, because longer sequences require more computation [4, 6]. To handle long-context inputs within limitations, some approaches make multiple calls to a pretrained model to leverage its in-context learning capabilities [5]. Additionally, context windows can theoretically grow as large as desired if there is enough memory available, though outputs may not make sense unless the model was trained on sequences of a similar length [6].

## 3. What were the main causes of the 2008 financial crisis?

The 2008 financial crisis was triggered by the collapse of the U.S. housing market and a banking crisis rooted in subprime mortgage lending [4, 5]. Key causes included excessive speculation on property values by both homeowners and financial institutions, which created a housing bubble [4, 6]. This was further exacerbated by predatory lending practices, loose credit, and regulatory deficiencies [4, 6]. Austrian school economists also attributed the crisis to the Federal Reserve's prolonged policy of maintaining artificially low interest rates [4]. Consequently, when mortgage-backed securities tied to these risky loans lost their value, major financial institutions failed and the credit markets froze [5].

## 4. What is the current state of fusion energy research?

Nuclear fusion research involves national and international collaboration projects, such as the ITER international megaproject, which aims to demonstrate the feasibility of magnetic confinement fusion using a tokamak reactor [4]. Additionally, the National Ignition Facility (NIF) is researching ways to maximize energy output for inertial confinement, while programs like ARPA-E's ALPHA have focused on advancing pulsed, intermediate-density fusion approaches to enable lower-cost pathways [1, 4]. Artificial intelligence also holds great potential to help advance fusion research through close collaborations between domain experts and AI developers [3]. Although research to put fusion energy on the grid is ongoing and has advanced plasma physics, some experts caution that claims of imminent commercial fusion energy in the next 10 to 20 years are overly hyped [5, 6].

## 5. How does CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing work at a molecular level?

CRISPR gene editing is a genetic engineering technique based on a simplified version of the bacterial CRISPR-Cas9 antiviral defense system [1]. At a molecular level, the method involves delivering the Cas9 nuclease complexed with a synthetic guide RNA (gRNA) into a cell [1]. This complex allows the cell's genome to be cut at a desired location [1]. Once the genome is cut at this specific site, existing genes can be removed or new genes can be added in vivo [1].
